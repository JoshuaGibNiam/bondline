Organic chemistry reagents cheat sheet

What each common reagent does, grouped by job. Learn the conversions, then test yourself on the synthesis problems.

Reactions · Updated 3 November 2026

Oxidising agents

ReagentWhat it does
K₂Cr₂O₇ / H₂SO₄ (acidified dichromate)Primary alcohol → aldehyde (distil) or carboxylic acid (reflux). Secondary alcohol → ketone. Orange → green.
PCC, CH₂Cl₂Primary alcohol → aldehyde (stops there). Secondary → ketone.
KMnO₄, cold and diluteAlkene → diol (syn addition of two OH).
KMnO₄, hotCleaves C=C; oxidises alkyl side chains on benzene to COOH.
O₃, then Zn/H₂O or (CH₃)₂SOzonolysis: cuts C=C into two C=O compounds.
mCPBAAlkene → epoxide.
Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺Oxidises aldehydes (silver mirror); no reaction with ketones.

Reducing agents

ReagentWhat it does
NaBH₄Aldehyde → primary alcohol; ketone → secondary alcohol. Doesn't reduce esters, acids or C=C.
LiAlH₄, then H₂OAlso reduces esters and carboxylic acids (→ primary alcohols), amides and nitriles (→ amines).
H₂, Pd/C (or Pt, Ni)C=C and C≡C → alkane.
H₂, Lindlar catalystAlkyne → cis (Z) alkene.
Na, NH₃(l)Alkyne → trans (E) alkene.
Sn, conc. HCl, then NaOHNitrobenzene → phenylamine.

Adding to alkenes

ReagentProduct
HBrBromoalkane, Markovnikov.
HBr, peroxidesBromoalkane, anti-Markovnikov.
Br₂1,2-Dibromide, anti addition. Decolourises bromine water.
H₂O, H₃PO₄ or H₂SO₄ catalystAlcohol, Markovnikov.
1. BH₃·THF 2. H₂O₂, NaOHAlcohol, anti-Markovnikov, syn.

Nucleophiles, bases and carbon–carbon bonds

ReagentWhat it does
NaOH(aq), warmHaloalkane → alcohol (substitution).
KOH in ethanol, hotHaloalkane → alkene (elimination).
KCN in ethanolHaloalkane → nitrile (adds one carbon).
HCN, KCN catalystAldehyde or ketone → hydroxynitrile (adds one carbon).
Excess NH₃ in ethanolHaloalkane → primary amine.
RMgBr (Grignard), then H₃O⁺With methanal → primary alcohol; other aldehydes → secondary; ketones → tertiary; CO₂ → carboxylic acid.
Ph₃P=CHR (Wittig)Aldehyde or ketone → alkene.
NaNH₂, then RXTerminal alkyne → longer alkyne.
LDAStrong, bulky base: makes enolates.
NaOEt or NaOMeStrong base / nucleophile: E2 with secondary and tertiary halides; Williamson ether synthesis with primary ones.

Changing OH and making acid derivatives

ReagentWhat it does
PBr₃ or SOCl₂Alcohol → bromoalkane or chloroalkane.
SOCl₂ (or PCl₅)Carboxylic acid → acyl chloride.
ROH, conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, heatCarboxylic acid → ester (Fischer esterification).
Conc. H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄, heatAlcohol → alkene (dehydration).
NaOH(aq), heatEster → carboxylate + alcohol (saponification).

Benzene

ReagentProduct
Conc. HNO₃, conc. H₂SO₄, < 55 °CNitrobenzene.
Br₂, FeBr₃ (or AlBr₃)Bromobenzene.
RCl, AlCl₃Alkylbenzene (Friedel–Crafts alkylation).
