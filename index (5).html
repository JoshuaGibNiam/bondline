IR spectroscopy table and how to read a spectrum

An IR spectrum tells you which functional groups are present. You only need a handful of absorptions, and the region above 1500 cm⁻¹ is where to look first.

Spectroscopy · Updated 3 November 2026

Telling carbonyl compounds apart

CompoundC=O / cm⁻¹ (approx.)Other clues
Acyl chlorideabout 1800highest C=O
Ester1735–1750strong C–O at 1000–1300
Aldehydeabout 1725C–H at about 2720 and 2820
Ketoneabout 1715no O–H, no aldehyde C–H
Carboxylic acidabout 1710very broad O–H, 2500–3300
Amide1650–1690N–H near 3300 (unless N is fully substituted)

How to read a spectrum in four checks

  1. Is there a strong peak at 1650–1820? Yes → there's a C=O.
  2. Is there a broad peak above 2500? Very broad from 2500–3300 with a C=O → carboxylic acid. Broad at 3200–3550 without C=O → alcohol.
  3. Anything at 2100–2260? → C≡N or C≡C.
  4. C–H just above or below 3000? Above → alkene or arene C–H. Only below → saturated.

The region below about 1500 cm⁻¹ is the fingerprint region: it's unique to each molecule, so it's used to match a compound against a known spectrum rather than to identify groups.
