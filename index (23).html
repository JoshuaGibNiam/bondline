Mass spectrometry: molecular ion, fragments and M+2 peaks

A mass spectrum gives you the molecular mass of a compound and clues to its structure from the way it breaks up. The peak furthest to the right (the molecular ion) is usually the first thing to find.

Spectroscopy · Updated 5 November 2026

The key peaks

  • Molecular ion, M⁺: the peak with the largest m/z (ignoring small M+1 and M+2 peaks). Its m/z is the relative molecular mass.
  • Base peak: the tallest peak, set to 100%. It's the most stable fragment, not necessarily the molecular ion.
  • M+1 peak: a small peak one unit above M⁺, from molecules containing a ¹³C atom. Its height is about 1.1% of M⁺ for every carbon, so the number of carbons ≈ (height of M+1 ÷ height of M⁺) × 100 ÷ 1.1.
  • M+2 peak: shows chlorine or bromine (see below).

Chlorine and bromine

HalogenIsotopesM⁺ : M+2Example

Bromine⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br, about 1:11 : 1bromoethane: m/z 108 and 110

Also look at the gaps from M⁺: a loss of 15 is CH₃, 18 is H₂O (common for alcohols), 29 is C₂H₅ or CHO, 31 is OCH₃, and 45 is COOH.

Worked examples

M⁺ = 72, base peak at 43, strong C=O in the IR. M = 72 fits C₄H₈O. A peak at 43 suggests CH₃CO⁺, and 72 − 43 = 29, a C₂H₅ group. So the compound is likely butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃.

The nitrogen rule

A molecule with an odd molecular mass contains an odd number of nitrogen atoms. If M⁺ is even, there are zero or two (or another even number of) nitrogens.

Quick answers

What is the molecular ion peak?

The molecular ion peak (M⁺) is the peak with the highest m/z value, apart from small isotope peaks. Its m/z equals the relative molecular mass of the compound.

What does an M+2 peak mean?

An M+2 peak two units above the molecular ion shows chlorine or bromine. Chlorine gives an M:M+2 ratio of about 3:1, and bromine about 1:1.

What is the base peak in mass spectrometry?

The base peak is the tallest peak in the spectrum, given a relative abundance of 100%. It comes from the most stable or most common fragment ion.
