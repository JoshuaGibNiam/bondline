Chapter 1

Amino acids are acids and bases at once

Every amino acid has an NH₂ (a base) and a COOH (an acid). In water they react with each other, so glycine exists as a zwitterion: + and − charges on the same molecule.

Low pH adds H⁺ (overall +1). High pH removes it (overall −1). At the isoelectric point the zwitterion dominates and the amino acid doesn’t move in an electric field.

Chapter 2

Joining amino acids

Scroll to bring glycine and alanine together. The COOH of one and the NH₂ of the other react, losing water. The new C(=O)–NH link is a peptide bond (an amide).

This is a condensation reaction. Hydrolysis (acid, or enzymes in your gut) reverses it.

Chapter 3

Primary structure

The sequence of amino acids, held by covalent peptide bonds. Orange beads are hydrophobic side chains, green are polar and red are charged.

Keep scrolling

Secondary structure

The backbone coils into an α-helix (or folds into β-sheets), held by hydrogen bonds between the C=O of one amino acid and the N–H four further along.

Keep scrolling

Tertiary structure

The whole chain folds up. Hydrophobic side chains pack into the middle, away from water. The shape is held by hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds and disulfide bridges (gold).

Try it

Denaturation

Heat the protein:

Above about 60 °C the weak interactions break and the protein unfolds. The primary structure survives, but the shape, and so the function, is lost. Extreme pH does the same by changing the charges.

Chapter 4

Enzymes are protein catalysts

Scroll to watch one cycle. The substrate binds in the active site, which closes around it (induced fit), strains its bonds and lowers the activation energy. The products leave and the enzyme is ready again.

Try it

Michaelis–Menten kinetics

Change the substrate concentration and add an inhibitor.

Km is the [S] that gives half of Vmax. A competitive inhibitor competes for the active site (more substrate overcomes it). A non-competitive one binds elsewhere and lowers Vmax.
