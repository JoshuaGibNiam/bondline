IChO: organic and biological chemistry

Organic and biological chemistry. Notes, syllabus points and practice questions for every unit.

Course · 15 units · Updated 21 November 2026

15 units that follow the International Chemistry Olympiad syllabus for organic chemistry and biochemistry. Each unit has the syllabus points, lessons, an olympiad-style problem with worked answers, and a quiz.

How the IChO syllabus works. Core topics (units 1–9) can appear in any IChO exam. Advanced topics (units 10–15) appear only if that year’s host country covers them in its preparatory problems, published early in the year, so check them to see which advanced units you need. A unit counts as complete when you have mastered 80% of its questions.

Unit 1: Structure and bonding free

53 questions

Hybridisation, σ and π bonds, resonance and aromaticity: the ideas every later unit uses to explain reactivity.

Count the atoms bonded to a carbon plus its lone pairs (its steric number). That number fixes the hybridisation and the shape.

Bond lengths follow: C–C 154 pm, C=C 134 pm, C≡C 120 pm. Every C–C bond in benzene is 139 pm, between a single and a double bond.

Syllabus points (4)
  • Hybridisation and geometry at carbon centres
  • σ and π bonds
  • Delocalisation and mesomeric (resonance) structures
  • Aromaticity (Hückel’s rule)

Lessons: Hybridisation and shape, σ and π bonds, Resonance (mesomeric) structures, Aromaticity, Worked problem: delocalisation and bond lengths, Antiaromaticity and Frost circles

Unit 2: Isomerism free

54 questions

Constitutional isomers, tautomers, stereoisomers and conformers. IChO problems often ask you to count or draw every isomer of a formula.

Isomers share a molecular formula. Constitutional (structural) isomers have their atoms joined in a different order. Stereoisomers have the same connections but a different arrangement in space: enantiomers, diastereomers, E/Z isomers. Conformers differ only by rotation about single bonds and interconvert rapidly at room temperature, so they aren’t separate compounds.

Bondline’s Stereo tab covers stereoisomers in depth, with 3D models you can turn. Use the links at the end of this unit.

Syllabus points (5)
  • Constitutional isomers
  • Configurational isomers: E/Z, cis/trans, R/S (CIP rules)
  • Conformations
  • Tautomerism
  • Chirality, optical activity and Fischer projections

Lessons: The kinds of isomer, Counting constitutional isomers, Tautomerism, Counting stereoisomers, Worked problem: counting all isomers, Keto–enol equilibria in numbers
