Atomic structure, bonding and periodicity

Unit 1: Atoms, isotopes and mass spectra free

Atomic structure; periodicity (Class 11) · 52 questions

What an atom is made of, why isotopes behave the same chemically, and how a mass spectrum gives a relative atomic mass.

Almost all the mass of an atom is in a tiny, dense nucleus of protons and neutrons. The electrons occupy the rest of the space, in shells around it. An atom is mostly empty space.

The proton number (atomic number, Z) is the number of protons. The nucleon number (mass number, A) is protons plus neutrons. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons; an ion has lost electrons (positive) or gained them (negative).

Syllabus points (3)
  • Subatomic particles; atomic number, mass number and isotopes
  • Atomic mass from isotopic abundance
  • Atomic and ionic radii (classification of elements and periodicity)

Lessons: Inside the atom, Relative masses and the mole, Isotopes, Relative atomic mass from a mass spectrum, How a mass spectrometer works, Isotope calculations both ways, Atomic and ionic radius, Radioactivity and nuclear reactions…

Unit 2: Electrons, orbitals and configurations free

Atomic structure (Class 11) · 64 questions

Shells, sub-shells and orbitals, the rules for filling them, and how to write the configuration of any atom or ion up to krypton.

Electrons occupy shells (principal quantum number n = 1, 2, 3 …). Each shell is split into sub-shells (s, p, d, f), and each sub-shell is made of orbitals. An orbital is a region of space that holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins.

Shapes: an s orbital is spherical. A p orbital is dumbbell-shaped, with two lobes either side of the nucleus; the three p orbitals (px, py, pz) point along three axes at right angles.

Syllabus points (5)
  • Electromagnetic radiation and the photoelectric effect
  • Spectrum of the hydrogen atom; Bohr model: postulates, radii, energies and limitations
  • Dual nature of matter: de Broglie relationship; Heisenberg uncertainty principle
  • Quantum numbers; shapes of s, p and d orbitals; nodes
  • Aufbau, Pauli and Hund; electronic configurations; stability of half-filled and filled orbitals

Lessons: Shells, sub-shells and orbitals, Filling order and the three rules, Configurations step by step, Blocks of the Periodic Table, Light, photons and the photoelectric effect, The Bohr model, dual nature and quantum numbers, The hydrogen emission spectrum, Unpaired electrons and magnetism

Unit 3: Ionisation energy free

Classification of elements and periodicity (Class 11) · 55 questions

Why it gets harder to remove electrons across a period, the two famous dips, and how successive ionisation energies reveal an element’s group.

The first ionisation energy is the energy needed to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms, forming one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.

Na(g) → Na⁺(g) + e⁻    ΔH = +496 kJ mol⁻¹
