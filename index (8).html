Nitrile from a halogenoalkane (SN2)

Cyanide attacks the δ+ carbon from behind as bromide leaves, in one step. The chain gains a carbon, and the nitrile can be hydrolysed to an acid or reduced to an amine.

Substitution · 1 step

Conditions

KCN or NaCN in ethanol, heat under reflux. Ethanol, not water, so that OH⁻ doesn’t compete.

Mechanism in 3 steps

Red curly arrows show where each pair of electrons moves.

About substitution reactions

In a substitution, a nucleophile replaces a leaving group on a saturated carbon. Whether it happens in one step (SN2) or through a carbocation (SN1) depends on the substrate, the nucleophile and the solvent.
