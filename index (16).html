Types of organic reactions, with examples

There are thousands of organic reactions, but only a few types. Name the type and you already know roughly what the mechanism looks like and what the product will be.

Reactions · Updated 5 November 2026

Substitution

One atom or group is replaced by another. In nucleophilic substitution, a nucleophile replaces a leaving group on a saturated carbon, such as hydroxide replacing bromide. It can happen in one step (SN2) or through a carbocation (SN1).

Electrophilic substitution happens on benzene rings: an electrophile replaces an H, so the ring stays aromatic. Nitration is the classic example. Free-radical substitution is how alkanes react with chlorine or bromine in UV light.

Addition

Two groups add across a double bond, and the double bond becomes single. Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition, such as with HBr, Br₂ or water. Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition, such as with NaBH₄ or HCN.

Elimination

The reverse of addition: a small molecule (HBr, H₂O) is removed from neighbouring carbons and a C=C forms. Strong bases and heat favour elimination over substitution. See SN1 vs SN2 vs E1 vs E2.

Condensation and hydrolysis

A condensation joins two molecules and releases a small one, usually water. Making an ester from an acid and an alcohol is a condensation, and so is making a polyamide. Hydrolysis is the reverse: water (often with acid or base) breaks a bond, such as an ester being split back into an acid and an alcohol.

Oxidation and reduction

In organic chemistry, oxidation usually means gaining bonds to oxygen or losing bonds to hydrogen: primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid, with acidified dichromate. Reduction is the reverse: NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes and ketones to alcohols, and LiAlH₄ also reduces esters and acids.

Acid–base reactions

A proton moves from an acid to a base. These are often the first step of a bigger mechanism, such as protonating an alcohol so water can leave.

Quick answers

What are the main types of organic reactions?

Substitution, addition, elimination, condensation, hydrolysis, oxidation, reduction and acid–base reactions. Most organic reactions are one of these.

What is the difference between substitution and addition?

In a substitution, one group replaces another, so the number of groups stays the same. In an addition, two groups add across a double bond and nothing is lost.

What is the difference between addition and elimination?

They are opposites. Addition adds atoms across a double bond and turns it into a single bond. Elimination removes atoms from neighbouring carbons and forms a double bond.
