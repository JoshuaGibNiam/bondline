All 20 amino acids

Each structure is drawn as the neutral L-amino acid, with the side chain (R group) shaded by type. In water at pH 7 they actually exist as zwitterions: NH₃⁺ and COO⁻.

pKa and pI values are typical textbook values (they vary slightly between sources and inside proteins). Essential = adults must get it from food: His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, Val.

The five side-chain groups

  • Nonpolar. Hydrocarbon side chains. They avoid water and pack inside folded proteins.
  • Aromatic. Side chains with a benzene-type ring. They absorb UV at 280 nm.
  • Polar, uncharged. OH, SH or amide side chains that hydrogen-bond with water.
  • Acidic (−). Carboxylic acid side chains, negatively charged at pH 7.
  • Basic (+). Amine-type side chains, positively charged at pH 7 (histidine only partly).

How to memorise them

  1. Learn the backbone once. Every amino acid is H₂N–CH(R)–COOH. Only the R group changes, so you're really memorising 20 side chains.
  2. Learn them in families. Ala → Val → Leu/Ile are just bigger and bigger hydrocarbons. Ser → Thr add an OH; Cys swaps the O of serine for S. Asp/Glu are acids, and Asn/Gln are their amides (one CH₂ apart each time).
  3. Pair the look-alikes. Phe + OH = Tyr. Asp + CH₂ = Glu. Ser with S instead of O = Cys.
  4. Use the odd one-letter codes as hooks: F = 'Fenylalanine', R = 'aRginine', Y = 'tYrosine', W = 'tWo rings' (tryptophan), Q = 'Q-tamine', D = 'asparDic', E = 'glutEmate', N = 'asparagiNe', K = just before L.
  5. Mnemonic for the essential nine: "PVT TIM HALL" – Phe, Val, Thr, Trp, Ile, Met, His, Arg*, Leu, Lys. (*Arg is only essential for children, so the adult list is nine.)
  6. Practise a little every day with the flashcards above, in both directions. Recall beats rereading.

Quick answers

Which amino acid is not chiral?

Glycine. Its side chain is a hydrogen atom, so the α-carbon has two identical H atoms and no stereocentre.

Why is L-cysteine R, when the other L amino acids are S?

L and D describe the 3D shape, which is the same for all of them. R and S follow CIP priorities. In cysteine, the CH₂SH group outranks COOH (sulfur beats oxygen), so the priorities swap and the label becomes R.

Which amino acids are charged at pH 7?

Aspartate and glutamate are negative. Lysine and arginine are positive. Histidine is only partly protonated, because its side-chain pKa is about 6.

What is the isoelectric point (pI)?

The pH at which the amino acid has no overall charge (all molecules are zwitterions). For a neutral side chain, pI is the average of the two backbone pKa values, about 2 and 9.5.
